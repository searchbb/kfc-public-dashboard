# OpenAI 暂停 Astra，因其接近首次「严重」网络安全风险

[p002]
Five days after OpenAI let mathematicians pick apart Astra's [proofs of ten long-open math problems](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/02/openais-astra-solves-10-long-open-math-problems-publishes-proofs/), the same model earned a much less celebratory distinction. Astra is now the first system OpenAI treats as potentially "Critical" for cybersecurity under its Preparedness Framework, the internal rulebook that decides how much containment a model needs before anyone gets to use it. The disclosure came in [a company post on Friday](https://openai.com/index/responding-next-frontier-critical-cyber-capabilities/), first reported by [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/07/openai-astra-model-delay-cybersecurity-risks), along with news that OpenAI has paused every internal Astra activity that does not yet meet a strengthened set of security controls.

[p003]
**The company under the most commercial pressure to ship its next model just told the market that the model may be able to break into hardened systems on its own.** That sentence deserves more attention than the delay it caused.

[p004]
## What Critical Actually Means

[p005]
OpenAI's framework reserves the Critical rating for a model that can do either of two things:

[p006]
Independently identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels against many hardened real-world critical systems, or devise and execute end-to-end novel strategies for cyberattacks against hardened targets given only a high-level desired goal.

[p007]
Every earlier frontier model, [including GPT-5.6-Sol, stayed in the High category](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/openai-locks-down-astra-after-model-raises-first-ever-critical-cyber-capability-fears). Astra's internal evaluations showed gains in autonomous coding and cybersecurity large enough that OpenAI says it can no longer rule the Critical rating out. Full benchmarking is still running, so this is a precaution rather than a verdict.

[p008]
The response is concrete either way. Astra now operates under [isolated testing environments, restricted network and tool access, encrypted model weights, sandboxed execution, and chain-of-thought monitoring that can interrupt high-risk activity in real time](https://www.iclarified.com/101698/openais-astra-model-hits-critical-cybersecurity-threshold-prompting-safety-pause). Government agencies and selected safety organizations get evaluation access before the public does. OpenAI still plans a broad release "once it satisfies the necessary safety and security requirements."

[p009]
## Two Weeks Of Converging Evidence

[p010]
The announcement landed in a stretch that gave it weight. On July 30, Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 documented [a single operator running a largely autonomous attack campaign](https://unit42.paloaltonetworks.com/autonomous-ai-cyber-attack-campaign/) against dozens of targets, with AI agents handling reconnaissance and exploitation work that once required a team. Three days before that, Microsoft [released its first in-house cybersecurity model](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/27/rethinking-security-for-the-age-of-ai/), a 5-billion-active-parameter specialist called [MAI-Cyber-1-Flash](https://microsoft.ai/news/introducing-mai-cyber-1-flash-inside-mdash/), built because renting general frontier models for defense work was no longer good enough.

[p011]
Ten days, three data points: a documented autonomous attack in the wild, a hyperscaler building specialist defense models, and a frontier lab declaring its first critical-class cyber capability. **The threat that security buyers have been treating as a planning scenario now has dated evidence attached from three independent directions.**

[p012]
## The Demand Signal

[p013]
A pause is the least interesting part of this announcement. The economics are sitting in the controls list. Encrypted weights, sandboxed execution, runtime monitoring of agent behavior, interrupt systems that halt an agent mid-task: OpenAI just published a working reference for what containing capable AI agents requires. The same problems arrive, at smaller scale, for any organization that puts capable agents into production, and the tooling to solve them is a product category taking shape in real time.

[p014]
OpenAI said as much in its post, writing that it is important to be transparent with the public and the security community "about this potential shift in capabilities." The framework has tiers for a reason, and for the first time since OpenAI built it, the top tier is in play.

[p015]
The capability also cuts both ways. A system that can independently find zero-day exploits can find them for the defender first, and OpenAI says it will hand third-party evaluators and government partners recommended controls built from its own containment work. **Offense reaching a threshold is simultaneously the strongest argument ever made for funding the defensive version of the same capability.** Microsoft reached that conclusion before the Astra news existed, which is why it built its own cyber model rather than waiting.

[p016]
## Where The Money Points

[p017]
The structural winners in this shift look familiar from the last month of news. Companies like CrowdStrike, which built its Charlotte AI around an agent-run security operations center, are selling into a problem that just grew a formal designation. So is Palo Alto Networks, whose research arm is producing the field evidence the industry now cites. Microsoft made itself both customer and vendor by taking model development in-house. The pattern across all three is the same: the security stack is being rearchitected around models fighting models, and the companies that own that architecture collect the spending that follows.

[p018]
How fast this moves will show up first at the other labs. Anthropic and Google DeepMind run comparable capability frameworks, and their next frontier releases will reveal whether Critical designations become routine or stay an OpenAI story. The government evaluations OpenAI invited will produce the first outside read on what Astra can actually do. And the cadence of defender-side model releases, from Microsoft and whoever follows it, will show whether defense is keeping pace with the offense curve or chasing it.

[p019]
The pause will lift, and Astra will ship. The capability it disclosed does not pause with it.
